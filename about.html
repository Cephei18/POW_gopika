I build systems because I'm obsessed with how the internet, people, and coordination actually work — and what breaks when you push them at scale.

Day to day I'm a computer science student at IIT Mandi, but the work lives somewhere between backend engineering and experimental internet architecture: autonomous ecosystem intelligence, Farcaster-native social systems, DeFi tooling, protocol mechanics, and AI agents that sound faintly absurd right up until they start working in production.

I'm drawn to the systems that run quietly underneath everything — async pipelines, real-time data flows, protocol integrations, caching layers, deterministic scoring. The unglamorous infrastructure that turns chaotic information into something a human can act on. That's the part I find beautiful.

Most of what I build starts as a stubborn little question:

can this be automated?
can coordination feel smarter?
can the internet behave more like an adaptive system?

Then I disappear — terminal windows, architecture sketches, Redis dashboards, and an indefensible number of open tabs — until the question turns into something real I can deploy.

When I'm not in the weeds, I'm building developer communities: mentoring students, running workshops, and dragging more people into technical spaces that used to feel locked. The coordination problem is the same one, just with humans instead of services.

I take the work seriously. I've just never believed serious people have to be boring about it.
